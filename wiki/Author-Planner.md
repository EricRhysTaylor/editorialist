Give your own writing goals a target date and room in your working week. Run **Open author planner**, or choose **Author planner** from the panel’s mode selector. This plan spans the vault and stays separate from each book’s [Editor Engagement](Editor-Engagement).

## Add a writing goal

1. Choose **Add goal**. Under **Goal**, enter a **Title**, and under **Done means** describe finishing in your own terms: a revised chapter, a finished query letter, or another outcome.
2. Under **Dates**, set a **Target date**. Optionally set a **Start** date, the day you plan to begin; it must fall on or before the target date. Check **Hard deadline** when scheduling must finish by that date.
3. Under **Book**, keep **Standalone goal** and optionally add a **Book label**, or choose a Radial Timeline book (see below).
4. Under **Word count**, optionally give the goal a number of words to write (see [Word targets](#word-targets)).
5. **More options** holds a **Linked document** to open from the card and a low and high effort in minutes for scheduling. Most goals leave them empty.
6. Save. **Edit goal** lets you change these details later.

Each goal card counts down to its date. Before a goal’s start date, it shows the days until you start and the length of the window; after that, the days left, plus the weeks when at least two weeks remain. A goal with a start date also shows a bar of how much of the time from start to deadline has passed. The bar measures time, not how much of the writing is done. It turns amber in the last 14 days and red when the goal is due today or past due, and a hard deadline’s date is shown in red.

A document link opens the working file. Linking or editing it never completes the goal. **Mark complete** records your decision; **Reopen goal** brings it back. **Remove goal** removes that goal and its planned sessions while preserving the document.

## Word targets

A goal can also count words, such as 50,000 words in November. In goal details, enter **Words to write** and choose a **Folder to count**. Every note in that folder and its subfolders counts. When the goal is linked to a Radial Timeline book, it counts the book’s source folder instead.

**Starting count** is filled in with the folder’s current total. Words written after that count toward the goal. If you already wrote some words for this goal before you set it up, lower the starting count by that amount. When the start date is still ahead, leave the field blank and the folder is counted on your start date.

The card shows the words written so far against the target, with a bar. Below it are the words written today and how many words a day finish the goal by its date. The count follows your writing as you type, and you don’t need to start a timer.

The count is the folder’s total, so cutting words lowers it, and moving a note into the folder raises it. Cut files saved by Editorialist are not counted. The day’s count starts the first time Obsidian is open that day. Reaching the target doesn’t complete the goal; **Mark complete** is still your decision.

## Schedule your goals

Choose **Schedule work** to open the planning dialog. Set a planning window, session length, and weekday capacity, then review **Preview plan** before **Use this plan** saves the sessions. Estimates are editable assumptions; use your own effort ranges when known. Goals are planned to their own dates; the cushion that the editor engagement keeps before an editor's deadline does not apply to them.

The **Schedule** tab shows **Queue** and **Days**, along with the forecast and available work. Target dates influence ordering; hard deadlines constrain placement. Sessions for the same goal stay ordered, while unrelated goals can still be scheduled if another goal cannot fit. Work that does not fit remains visible as unscheduled.

Marking a session **Done** from its circle records work on that session. Use **Mark complete** on the goal when its outcome is achieved. The two planners have their own capacity settings and sessions; do not assume one reserves time in the other. **Today** adds up both: when the day's sessions need more time than either planner sets aside for that day, it says so, so you can move some to another day.

## Optional Radial Timeline target dates

In goal details, under **Book**, choose a **Radial Timeline book** and its **Stage**: Zero draft, Author draft, House draft, or Press ready. You can choose any available Radial Timeline book, independently of the active book.

The goal shares that stage’s target date. Changing the goal’s date updates Radial Timeline; a date changed there appears here while the panel is open. Stage dates must retain their chronological order. If the stage date moves before the goal’s start date, the card says so in place of the countdown; edit the goal to choose a new start. Completing a goal records your outcome without changing the manuscript’s publishing status.

If RT, the linked book, or its stage date is unavailable, the card keeps its last synced date and shows the problem. Scheduling pauses for that linked goal until you reconnect, set the stage date, or unlink it. Standalone goals continue to work without RT. Removing a goal or unlinking it preserves the RT target date.

## Storage

Goals, sessions, and scheduling defaults are saved locally in Editorialist’s plugin data across the vault. Document links are optional. No AI, calendar account, or external scheduling service is required to use the planner.
